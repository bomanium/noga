# RGB gamut

Asked 2026-11-06, when the owner took the backlog's next Next item after constant colour: pixels whose R, G or B falls
outside EBU R 103's -5% to 105%, the 2026-11-03 review's fourth pick (decisions/2026-11-05-av-lengths.md). FFmpeg has
no filter that counts them, and its RGB conversions (format=gbrp) clip, which hides them.

## What the figure is checked against

- **geq's arithmetic** (recommended): in the tests, FFmpeg's geq filter writes out BT.709 (BT.601 below 720 lines)
  per pixel and marks each one outside -5% to 105%; signalstats' YAVG of that mask is the frame's share. Exact, all in
  FFmpeg, slow only in the tests. Chroma is taken from the sample beside each pixel, as 4:2:2 is sited, not
  interpolated.
- zscale to float RGB: zimg's conversion (zscale to gbrpf32le), unclipped. A different library's matrix and
  interpolated chroma, and no FFmpeg filter then counts float pixels, so the test would read the frames out itself.
- Not against FFmpeg: only Noga's own arithmetic, tested on hand-made frames of known colours; the report says so, as
  for the shot list.

**Answer: geq's arithmetic.**

## How it is judged

- **Like broadcast range** (recommended): R 103's 1% area allowance. A frame with over 1% of its pixels out of gamut
  counts, spans under 1 s apart are joined, each span is a warning with its worst frame's share, bars are left out;
  otherwise a pass row with the worst frame. Profile files change the share and the limits (`maxOutOfGamut`,
  `gamutLow`, `gamutHigh`). EBU QC item 0051B, as range.
- A failure: as the first, but each span fails the file.
- Information only: reported with its share and spans, never a warning, since gamut is often a soft limit.

**Answer: like broadcast range.**

## The samples

The check found a fault in make-samples.sh's programmes: testsrc2 draws its colours straight into YUV with BT.601's
matrix, but the samples are 1080p, tagged BT.709 (DNxHR, H.264) or untagged (ProRes), so they read as BT.709. Read
that way, 64% of testsrc2's picture was out of gamut for its 9 s (0.5% read as BT.601), and every sample programme,
and the README's example report, would have warned.

- **Make them BT.709** (recommended): make-samples.sh draws testsrc2 in RGB and converts it with BT.709 (format=rgb24,
  then scale's out_color_matrix=bt709), so the programmes are what a real HD master is and pass gamut. The samples
  regenerate on their own (the script's SHA-256); any test pinned to testsrc2's old YUV values is updated, and the
  example report is redone (it gains a gamut row either way).
- Keep them, the warning standing: the example report shows a gamut warning, a realistic case of a BT.601 picture
  in a BT.709 file.

**Answer: make them BT.709.**

## What was built

- `GamutMeter` (Noga.Ffmpeg), fed every decoded frame's planes on both paths through `FrameMeters`, on a thread of its
  own. Y from 0 (black) to 1 (white) and Cb and Cr from -0.5 to 0.5 (limited range 16-235 and 16-240 scaled to the
  depth; full range 0 to 2^depth - 1). Then R = Y + 2(1 - Kr)Cr, B = Y + 2(1 - Kb)Cb, G = (Y - Kr R - Kb B) / Kg,
  with Kr and Kb from the file's matrix tag (bt709; bt470bg or smpte170m; bt2020nc), else BT.709, or BT.601 under 720
  lines. A pixel is out of gamut when any of the three is below `Profile.GamutLow` (-0.05) or above `GamutHigh`
  (1.05). Each pixel takes the Cb and Cr sample it is sited on (the one to its left in 4:2:2 and 4:2:0, the row above
  in 4:2:0), as the geq expression does.
- Each of R, G and B rises with Y by the same step for a given Cb and Cr, so the codes in gamut are one run of Y for
  each pair: a table of those runs (64 K entries at 8 bits, 1 M at 10), its edges found by the same arithmetic, makes a
  pixel one lookup. The table takes about 50 ms at 10 bits.
- `Measurements.OutOfGamut` (spans over `Profile.MaxOutOfGamut`, 1%, bars left out, under `RangeMergeGap` apart
  joined) and `WorstOutOfGamut`; `gamut` findings: a warning per span ("Pixels outside RGB gamut (-5% to 105%): up to
  64.5% of the picture (over 1%) for 9.00 s"), else a pass with the worst frame. EBU QC item 0051B (Video Signal
  Levels), which covers gamut as well as levels.
- Checked against `ffmpeg -vf geq=lum='...':cb=MID:cr=MID,signalstats,metadata=print` (`FfmpegCli.GamutShares`): the
  arithmetic above as one geq expression, in the same order, marking each pixel 0 or the depth's maximum; YAVG over that
  maximum is the frame's share. On generated FFV1 files (8-bit 4:2:2 and 4:2:0, 10-bit 4:2:2: grey, a chroma ramp,
  a 0.5% patch) every frame's share within signalstats' printing and the spans exact (`AssertGamut`). On
  make-samples.sh's 1080p programmes geq takes some 3 minutes for 20 s, so every 25th frame is compared
  (`AssertGamutFrames`), and the managed path's spans and worst frame are the FFmpeg path's.
- make-samples.sh: testsrc2 through RGB and BT.709, straight to 4:2:2 10-bit; the programme's other parts in 4:2:2
  10-bit too, so that concat converts none of them; H.264's 4:2:0 bilinear. Bicubic chroma resampling overshoots
  testsrc2's saturated edges outside broadcast range: 2-5% of the picture, against 0.15% (ProRes), 0.5% (DNxHR) and
  0.87% (H.264) now. The programmes' gamut: 0.34%, 0.24% and 0.96%. (A filter graph's `sws_flags=bilinear;` prefix
  lost the H.264 file's timecode, and swscale's bilinear RGB to YUV ignores out_range, so neither is used.)
- On make-samples.sh's 20 s programmes (14 s of bars and black), Release, 4 cores, the two meters of 2026-11-06
  together (constant colour and gamut) take a check from 2.9 to 3.5 s (DNxHR), 3.9 to 4.4 s (H.264) and 3.9 to 4.6 s
  (ProRes).
