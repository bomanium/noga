# The app icon

Question: which icon for the desktop app, `mediaqc-app`. Five concepts were drawn as plain SVG (no fonts, no
third-party artwork), rendered at 256, 48, 32 and 16 px, and shown to the owner on a page with the research and the
trade-offs. Three were the ideas already floated (B, C, D), two were new (A, E).

What the research found: the desktop tools in this space draw from a few motifs that many unrelated apps share (film
strips and clapperboards, waveforms and meters, a white tick on a green circle, magnifiers); colour bars are a public
test signal, nobody's mark. Small sizes want one silhouette with strong contrast on light and dark taskbars, and no
text. Windows takes 16, 24, 32, 48 and 256 px frames in an .ico (an exact-size frame wins); freedesktop asks for at
least a 48×48 PNG in hicolor, an SVG in `hicolor/scalable/apps` covers every size; GNOME draws on a 128 px canvas and
adds a monochrome symbolic icon; KDE's colourful app icons are 48 px. Avalonia's `Window.Icon` takes an .ico or PNG,
not SVG.

Choices:

1. A: bars in a check: a tick cut out of the seven colour bars, on a dark tile (recommended). One silhouette at every
   size; reads as a colourful tick at 16 px.
2. B: colour bars with a white tick on a green badge. Clear at 48 px and up; the badge is the generic "verified"
   look, and at 16 px it is a striped square.
3. E: a green tick inside a viewfinder's corner marks. The cleanest at 16 px, but looks like screenshot and scanner
   tools and says nothing about broadcast.
4. C (a film frame with a green waveform and a tick) or D (a magnifier over a frame). C turns to noise at 32 px and
   below; D reads as "search".

Answer: 1.

With the code: `src/MediaQc.App/Assets/icon.svg`, the source for every size. The window icon, the Windows executable
icon, the script that makes the PNGs and the .ico from the SVG (under `tools/`) and the Linux .desktop entry come in a
separate pull request (branch `claude/app-icon-desktop`). This container's ImageMagick has no SVG reader (no
`rsvg-convert`); the concept renders were made with Chromium.
